## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What insurance policies should every household have beyond the basics like auto, home and health?

After years as an independent broker, my short answer is that most households are missing three policies: an umbrella, disability income coverage, and flood insurance. Most of the rest of what gets pitched is optional.

Umbrella liability comes first. Your home and auto policies carry liability limits, often $300,000 or $500,000, and a serious claim can blow past that. I had a client whose son's friend was hurt on a backyard trampoline, and the medical bills passed $300,000 almost immediately. An umbrella adds a large layer of liability coverage, often $1 million or more, for a relatively modest annual premium. It matters most if you have a pool, a trampoline, a dog, teen drivers, or significant assets to protect.

Second is disability income insurance. People insure their cars carefully and ignore their ability to earn a paycheck. If you can't work for six months or two years, your mortgage and bills don't pause. Check what your employer provides, because group policies are often limited and may be taxable when paid. Then decide whether you need a supplemental individual policy.

Third is flood insurance. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage. Many people assume that if they are not in a mapped high-risk zone, they are safe, but a large share of flood claims come from outside those zones. There is also usually a waiting period before a new policy takes effect, so you can't buy it when a storm is already approaching.

What you can usually skip or evaluate carefully: extended warranties, credit life insurance, and many narrow add-ons that overlap with coverage you already have.

To see where your own gaps are, pull out your declarations pages and look at your liability limits and exclusions. I put together a longer breakdown of these three policies here for additional reading: https://siavashsed.github.io/insightinsure/insurance-policies-you-should-have/
