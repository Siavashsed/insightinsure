## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Did the FCA's 2022 ban on insurance price walking actually save UK customers money, and do I still need to shop around at renewal?

Short answer: it helped, but it didn't make shopping around unnecessary.

Before 1 January 2022, many UK insurers routinely charged loyal customers more at renewal than they'd quote a brand-new customer for the identical home or motor policy. This was price walking, and it quietly cost people a lot over the years. The FCA rule now bars insurers from renewing you at a price higher than the equivalent new-customer price for that same product through the same channel.

I'm an independent broker, and I remember sitting with a retired bus driver in Leeds just before the ban started, going through a home renewal that had crept up year after year. For people like him, the rule is a genuine win. The worst of the loyalty penalty is gone.

Here's what it doesn't do:

1. It doesn't make your renewal the cheapest option. It only caps it against that insurer's own new-customer price. A competitor may still quote far less.
2. It doesn't stop insurers adjusting new-customer pricing. Some have repriced the whole book, so the gap has narrowed in places rather than vanishing.
3. It doesn't protect you if cover quietly changes. Check the excess, limits and add-ons each year.

My practical advice: get at least three comparable quotes about three to four weeks before renewal, make sure the cover is like for like, and ask your current insurer to match the best one. Often they will.

The ban raised the floor for consumers. Comparing quotes is still how you find the best deal.
