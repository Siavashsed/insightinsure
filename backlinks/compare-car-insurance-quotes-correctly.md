## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I compare car insurance quotes correctly so I don't end up with less coverage than I thought?

The most reliable way to compare car insurance quotes is to make every quote describe the same policy before you look at a single price. Most people do it backwards. They line up a few numbers, choose the lowest, and discover what they bought only when they file a claim.

Here is the process I walk my clients through as an independent broker.

1. Write down your target coverage first. Pick your liability limits, your collision and comprehensive deductibles, and any extras you want, such as uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage, rental reimbursement, roadside assistance and glass coverage.

2. Ask every company to quote that exact setup. If a quote comes back with a different limit or deductible, it isn't comparable. Send it back or adjust it.

3. Check the deductibles carefully. A lower premium often hides a $1,500 deductible where another quote has $500. That difference is real money on the day you need it.

4. Look at liability limits. State minimums are low enough that a single serious accident can exceed them, leaving you personally responsible for the rest.

5. Ask what's excluded. Accident forgiveness, how total-loss payouts are valued, and whether rental coverage is capped per day can matter more than a $20 monthly difference.

6. Only then compare prices. With identical coverage, the cheaper quote is genuinely cheaper.

A neighbor of mine skipped this and saved $38 a month, then found out five months later, after a claim, that her new policy covered much less. Her savings were gone in one afternoon.
