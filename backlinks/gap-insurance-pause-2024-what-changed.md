## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I buy GAP insurance from the car dealer when financing a car in the UK?

Short answer: usually not from the dealer, although GAP itself can be worth having.

I'm an independent insurance broker, and people ask me this a lot. GAP (guaranteed asset protection) covers the difference between what your insurer pays out if the car is written off or stolen and what you paid for it, or what you still owe on the finance. In the first two or three years, when cars lose value fastest, that difference can be thousands of pounds.

The problem is where and how it's sold. In 2024 the FCA paused dealer sales of GAP after its review found that on some products, less than 10p of every pound paid in premiums went back out in claims. Most of the money went on commission. Dealers are now allowed to sell it again, but the incentives at the showroom desk are still there.

Before you sign anything, I'd suggest:

1. Ask for the GAP premium as a single price, not folded into your monthly payment. If it's added to the finance, you pay interest on it.
2. Get a standalone quote online. In my experience these often cost a fraction of the dealer price for similar cover.
3. Check which type of policy it is. Return-to-invoice, finance GAP and vehicle replacement cover different things.
4. Check your motor policy. Some include new-car replacement in the first year, which overlaps with GAP.
5. Don't let yourself be rushed. Many standalone providers let you buy cover within a set window after you collect the car.

So when is GAP worth it? Usually when you have a new or nearly new car, a small deposit and a long finance term. If you paid cash for a five-year-old car, it probably isn't.
