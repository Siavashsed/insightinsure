## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What does renters insurance actually cover besides my personal belongings?

Most people assume renters insurance is just a policy for your furniture and electronics. In my experience as an independent broker, that's the smallest part of its value. A standard policy has three main parts, and the other two often matter more.

1. Personal property. This is the part everyone knows. If a fire, theft or water damage destroys your belongings, the policy pays to repair or replace them. Check whether you have replacement cost or actual cash value, because the difference can be thousands of dollars.

2. Liability coverage. If a guest is injured in your home, or you accidentally cause damage to the unit next door, you can be sued. Liability coverage pays for your legal defense and any judgment, typically up to $100,000 or $300,000. Lawsuits can run far past what most renters have in savings, so this is the piece I'd least want to go without.

3. Loss of use. If a covered event makes your apartment unlivable, the policy pays for a hotel, meals beyond your normal food costs, and other temporary living expenses. I once had a client whose upstairs neighbor's washer hose burst. Her furniture loss was about $4,000, but her hotel and living expenses ended up bigger than that.

One more thing people miss: your belongings are usually covered away from home too. A bike stolen from outside a coffee shop or a laptop taken from your car can often be claimed, subject to your deductible and any sublimits.

All of this typically costs $10 to $20 a month. Before you buy, make sure you understand your deductible, your liability limit, and whether valuables like jewelry need a separate rider.
