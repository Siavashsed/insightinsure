**Title:** Is it worth staying with a captive agent once your situation gets more complicated?

Something I wish someone had explained to me years ago: there are basically three ways to buy insurance, and they are not equal.

A captive agent works for one company. However friendly they are, they can only quote you that one company's policies. If that carrier's pricing has drifted up for your profile, they have nothing else to offer you.

Buying direct online can be the cheapest option, but mostly when your situation is simple: one car, clean record, renting. Once you add a house, a teen driver, a claim or two, or a small business, the online quote engines get a lot less forgiving.

An independent broker can shop several carriers at once. For anything beyond the basics, that usually wins, because the same profile can price very differently from one company to the next.

My takeaway: if you have been with the same captive agent for years and your life has gotten more complicated, get at least one broker quote to compare. Worst case, you confirm you are already in a good spot.

Curious whether others have found the same.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I use an independent insurance broker, a captive agent, or buy insurance directly from the company?

I am an independent broker, so keep that in mind when you weigh my answer. Even so, the honest answer depends on how complicated your situation is.

Buying direct, online or by phone with the insurer, often wins on price when your profile is simple and clean: one car, a rented apartment, no recent claims or tickets. The quote engines are built for that customer, and you skip the middleman.

A captive agent works for one insurance company. Many are knowledgeable and genuinely helpful, but they can only sell you that company's policies. If their carrier happens to price your profile well, great. If not, they have nowhere else to send you. In my experience captive agents rarely win on price and only sometimes win on service.

An independent broker can quote several carriers for the same coverage. That matters most once your life gets more complex: a house, multiple vehicles, a teen driver, a past claim, a home business, or retirees on a fixed income. Insurers price these risks very differently, and the gap between the cheapest and most expensive quote for the same coverage can be large.

A practical approach:

1. If your situation is simple, get a direct quote first.
2. If it is anything more involved, get at least one broker quote and compare it against your current policy line by line, not just on premium. Check deductibles, liability limits and exclusions.
3. Recheck every couple of years or after a big life change. Rates drift, and loyalty is rarely rewarded at renewal.

Whichever route you choose, compare the coverage, not only the price.
