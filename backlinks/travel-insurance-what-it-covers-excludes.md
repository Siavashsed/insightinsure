**Title:** What's the one thing you assumed your travel insurance covered that it didn't?

I've worked as an independent insurance broker for years, and the same misunderstanding comes up again and again. People buy travel insurance to cover the thing they're worried about, and that is often the thing it excludes.

The basic rule is that a policy pays for sudden, unexpected, documented events that are written into the wording. It does not pay if you change your mind, get nervous about a destination, or the airline messes you around in a way the policy doesn't list. Pre-existing conditions, 'fear of travel' cancellations and some delay scenarios are the usual surprises.

One case has stuck with me. A client rang me from a hospital abroad at 6:40 in the morning because her husband had a kidney stone and the bill was heading toward four figures and beyond. Having the policy was not what mattered that morning. What mattered was whether she had read the medical section, knew the emergency assistance number, and had kept every receipt and report. Those small steps decided how smoothly the claim went.

My advice is to read the exclusions page before the benefits page, and to ask what 'unexpected' means in your policy. Cancel-for-any-reason cover costs more but exists for exactly the worry people actually have.

What has caught you out? I'd like to hear other people's claim stories.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What does travel insurance actually cover, and what does it usually exclude?

I'm an independent insurance broker, and the short answer is this: travel insurance covers sudden, unexpected, documented events that are written into your policy, and almost nothing else.

What it usually covers:
- Emergency medical treatment abroad, including hospital stays and, on better policies, evacuation or repatriation
- Trip cancellation or interruption for listed reasons, such as serious illness, a death in the family or jury service
- Lost, stolen or damaged luggage, within per-item limits
- Some delays, once you pass a waiting period set in the wording

What it usually excludes:
- Changing your mind, or being nervous about travelling
- Pre-existing medical conditions you did not declare, or that were not stable when you bought the policy
- Injuries linked to alcohol or to activities the policy classes as hazardous
- Events that were already known or foreseeable when you bought cover, such as a storm that already had a name
- Airline failures or poor service that the policy does not list

The gap between those two lists catches people out more than anything else. I once took a call from a client in a Lisbon hospital at 6:40 in the morning. Her husband had a kidney stone and the bill was climbing fast. The claim went well because she had the assistance number, had kept every document, and had read the medical section beforehand.

My advice:
1. Read the exclusions before the benefits.
2. Declare every medical condition, even if it seems minor.
3. Check the limits per item and per claim, not only the headline figure.
4. If your worry is cancelling for personal reasons, ask about cancel-for-any-reason cover. It costs more, but it is built for that.
5. Save the emergency number offline and keep receipts for everything.
