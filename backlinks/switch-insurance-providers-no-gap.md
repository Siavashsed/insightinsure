**Title:** Anyone else learn the hard way that you shouldn't cancel the old policy until the new one is in writing?

I'm an independent broker, and the most common coverage gap I clean up has the same cause: someone cancels first and plans to start the new policy later. One client cancelled his auto policy by phone at 4:50 on a Friday and planned to start the new one Monday morning. A deer hit his Camry on Sunday night, and he had nothing in force.

The rule I give everyone is simple. Don't cancel anything until the new policy is confirmed in writing, with a start date that's the same day or earlier than your old policy's end date. A verbal quote doesn't count. A payment that's still processing doesn't count either. You want a declarations page or a binder letter in your inbox.

A few other things that help:
- Overlapping by a day or two costs very little, and a gap can cost you tens of thousands.
- Ask the old carrier for a written cancellation date, not just a phone confirmation.
- Check that the new policy covers everything the old one did, including rental reimbursement and any lienholder requirements.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I switch insurance companies without a gap in coverage?

I'm Patrick Lane, an independent insurance broker, and I've cleaned up more coverage gaps than I'd like. Nearly all of them come from one mistake: cancelling the old policy before the new one is confirmed.

Here is the order I tell clients to follow.

1. Get the new policy bound first. Don't cancel anything yet. Ask for a declarations page or a binder letter by email. A verbal quote or a "your payment went through" screen isn't proof of coverage.

2. Check the effective date. The new policy should start on the same day your old one ends, or earlier. If you're unsure, pick an earlier start date. Overlapping by a day or two usually costs a few dollars. A gap can cost you far more.

3. Compare coverage line by line. Make sure limits, deductibles, rental reimbursement, roadside assistance and any lienholder or mortgage requirements match what you had before, or are better.

4. Then cancel the old policy, and get the cancellation in writing. Ask for the exact date and time it ends. Phone confirmations disappear.

5. Watch for refunds and notices. Your old insurer owes you any unused premium. Also tell your lender if you have a loan or mortgage, because they'll want proof of the new policy.

I once had a client cancel his auto policy by phone at 4:50 on a Friday, planning to start the new one Monday. A deer hit his car on Sunday night and he had nothing in force. That's the kind of thing this process prevents.
