## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I buy gap insurance from the car dealer when buying a new car?

Short answer: if you're buying new on a small deposit, gap insurance is usually worth having. Buying it from the dealer usually isn't.

I'm an independent broker, and I see the same pattern again and again. Last March I sat with a client in a Peugeot showroom outside Reading. At about 6pm, once the finance was agreed, the business manager offered a three-year gap policy at £649. In the car park afterwards I found a standalone return-to-invoice policy for the same car and the same term at £162.

Why gap cover matters: if your car is written off or stolen, your motor insurer pays its market value at the time of the loss. New cars lose value quickly in the first couple of years, so that payout can fall well short of what you paid or what you still owe on finance. Gap insurance covers the difference.

Why the dealer price is often high: the policy is sold at the end of a long process, attached to the finance conversation, and few buyers compare it on the spot. Dealers also earn commission on it.

What I'd do:

1. Decide which type you need. Return-to-invoice pays back to what you paid; finance gap clears the outstanding loan; vehicle replacement covers a new equivalent car.
2. Check the claim limit against your car's price. A cheap policy with a low cap may not cover a large shortfall.
3. Ask the dealer for their quote in writing, then get two or three standalone quotes.
4. Check the purchase window. Most standalone providers let you buy within a set period after delivery, so you don't need to decide in the showroom.

If you're paying a large deposit or cash and plan to sell within a year or two, the case for gap cover is weaker.
