## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do insurance companies calculate my premium, and what can I actually do to lower it?

Your premium is the insurer's estimate of what you'll cost them in claims, plus their overhead and profit. That estimate comes from a rating formula that you never get to see. You can't edit most of it, but you can change enough of the inputs to make a real difference.

The inputs fall into two groups. The first group is hard to change: your age, your claims history, the vehicle or property you're insuring, and in most states a credit-based insurance score. The second group is where I focus with clients, because these are the ones you can influence.

The first is accuracy of the data on file. Annual mileage, garaging ZIP code, vehicle use and the listed drivers are often wrong or years out of date. An old commute figure can add hundreds of dollars a year.

The second is coverage structure. Raising a deductible to a level you could comfortably cover from savings lowers the premium. So does dropping collision or comprehensive on an older car that isn't worth much.

The third is discounts. Many are not applied automatically. Bundling, paid-in-full, telematics, safe-driver status and professional or alumni affiliations are all worth asking about by name.

The fourth is shopping. Insurers weigh the same factors differently, so the same driver can get quotes that are far apart. Compare at renewal, not only after a rate increase.

A real example: in March 2023 I reviewed a Tulsa client who was paying $2,340 a year for a 2019 Camry with no claims. The cause was in how he was being rated, not in his record. Correcting the inputs changed the outcome.

If you want the longer version, I broke down the whole calculation in this article: https://siavashsed.github.io/insightinsure/how-insurers-calculate-your-premium-lower-it/

The short version is to audit your inputs once a year. It takes about an hour and it's often the highest-return hour in your household budget.
