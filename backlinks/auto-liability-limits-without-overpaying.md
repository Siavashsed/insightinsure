**Title:** Does anyone actually know what happens if you cause an accident while carrying only state minimum liability?

I used to assume state minimum liability was the 'responsible' baseline. If the law requires it, it must be enough, right? It isn't. Those minimums were set to keep cars legally on the road. They were never meant to protect you.

Here's the problem. Say you rear-end a newer car and the other driver needs medical care. The bills can blow through a low property damage limit and a low per-person injury limit very quickly. Once your policy pays its maximum, the rest is your problem. That can mean a lawsuit, a judgment, and in many states wage garnishment until it's paid off.

What surprised me is how cheap the fix usually is. Going from the minimum to 100/300/100 often costs less per month than a streaming subscription. If you own a home or have real savings, it's worth going higher and pricing an umbrella policy, which tends to be cheaper than people expect.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is carrying only the state minimum auto liability insurance a bad idea?

For most people, yes. I say that as an independent broker who has watched it go wrong for real clients.

State minimums exist to keep a car legally on the road. They were never designed to cover what a moderate accident actually costs today. Many states still set property damage minimums in the single or low double digits of thousands of dollars. Plenty of cars on the road cost several times that to repair or replace, and a single ER visit with follow-up care can use up a low per-person injury limit.

When your policy hits its limit, the insurer stops paying and you are personally responsible for the rest. Depending on your state, that can lead to a lawsuit, a judgment against you, and wage garnishment for years.

I saw this up close in 2019. A client of mine in Sacramento was carrying California's old 15/30/5 minimum when she rear-ended a Tesla. The coverage gap became her problem, not her insurer's. (California has since raised its minimum to 30/60/15, which is better but still thin.)

My general guidance:

1. Most drivers should carry at least 100/300/100. That means $100k per injured person, $300k per accident and $100k for property damage.
2. The price difference is usually small. Moving up from the minimum often costs less per month than a streaming subscription.
3. If you own a home, have meaningful savings or earn a good income, go higher and look at a $1M umbrella policy. Umbrellas are surprisingly affordable once your underlying auto limits meet the insurer's requirements.
4. Match uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage to your liability limits, because the other driver may be the one carrying the minimum.
