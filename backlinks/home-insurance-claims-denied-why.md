## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do home insurance companies deny claims, and what can I do about it?

Having worked as an independent insurance broker and consumer advocate, I can tell you most denials aren't dramatic. They almost always fall into three buckets.

1. Damage the policy never covered. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood, earth movement, and many forms of sewer backup unless you've added endorsements. This is a coverage gap, not a claims-handling failure, and the time to catch it is before you need to file.

2. Damage the insurer calls neglect. This is the gray zone. Insurers often deny water damage as long-term seepage or lack of maintenance. I once sat with a client in Tulsa holding a $14,300 denial for a burst pipe under her sink, where the adjuster used exactly that language. Her plumber's invoice and dated photos were sitting in a folder. They'd never been submitted. Evidence of a sudden event versus gradual wear is often the entire case.

3. Paperwork errors or delays. Missed proof-of-loss deadlines, incomplete inventories, and vague descriptions of loss sink more claims than people realize.

What you can do:

- Document before you clean up: photos, video, dates.
- Report promptly and keep a log of every call, with names and times.
- Request the denial in writing, citing the specific policy language.
- Respond to that exact reason in your appeal, with evidence.
- Ask for a second adjuster or independent inspection if the findings seem off.
- If it's still stuck, file a complaint with your state's department of insurance. It costs nothing and insurers take it seriously.

A public adjuster or attorney can make sense on larger losses, but many denials turn around with better documentation alone.
