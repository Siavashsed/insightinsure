**Title:** PSA: the other driver's apology at the scene is worth almost nothing to your claim. Photos are what count.

Here's something I keep seeing in threads here. Someone gets into a minor crash, and the other driver is apologetic and says their insurance will cover it. So nobody takes real photos. A week later the story changes, and it's one person's word against the other's.

An apology at the curb doesn't count for much. Adjusters decide claims on evidence. Here's what actually helps:

- Wide shots of both cars where they stopped, before anyone moves them, showing the lanes, signals and signs
- Close-ups of every damaged area, plus the other car's plate
- A photo of their license and insurance card instead of copying it down by hand
- Names and numbers of any witnesses, even just one
- A quick voice memo of what happened while you still remember it clearly

It takes about five minutes, and it can decide whether you get a clean claim or a fight over fault. A couple of blurry photos of a bumper usually aren't enough.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What should I do right after a car accident to protect my insurance claim?

As an independent broker, the most common claim problem I see isn't fraud or a stubborn adjuster. It's missing evidence from the first few minutes after the crash.

This is the order I'd follow:

1. Check for injuries first. Call 911 if anyone is hurt or the cars are blocking traffic. Safety always comes before paperwork.

2. Take photos before anything moves. Get wide shots from several angles showing both cars, the lane markings, traffic lights and signs. Then take close-ups of every damaged area on both vehicles, with the other car's plate in frame.

3. Photograph documents instead of writing them down. That means the other driver's license, insurance card and registration. Handwritten notes get copied wrong.

4. Find witnesses. One neutral person's name and phone number can settle a dispute over fault.

5. Record your account while you still remember it clearly. A one-minute voice memo on your phone covering the time, which way each car was going, your speed and what you saw is enough.

6. Stay polite, but don't argue about fault at the scene. Don't admit fault, and don't count on the other driver's admission either. "My insurance will handle it" carries very little weight once their insurer hears a different version.

7. Report the accident to your own insurer promptly, even if you expect the other side to pay. Most policies require timely notice.

I've watched a simple rear-end claim turn into weeks of back and forth because the client had two blurry photos and a verbal promise.
