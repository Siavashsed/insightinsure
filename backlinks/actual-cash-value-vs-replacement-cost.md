**Title:** Check your policy for 'actual cash value' before hail season. It matters more than your deductible on a big claim

Quick PSA for anyone with an older roof, or a homeowners policy you haven't read in a while. Your declarations page and endorsements will say either 'actual cash value' (ACV) or 'replacement cost' (RC). On a large claim, that wording matters more than your deductible.

ACV pays what the damaged item was worth used, after depreciation. RC pays what it costs to replace it new. Roofs depreciate fast because insurers treat them as wear items with a set lifespan, so roof claims are where the gap gets big.

Here's a real example. A 17-year-old roof in Edmond, OK got destroyed by golf-ball hail in 2022. The replacement estimate was $21,400 and the ACV check was $7,240. It was the same roof, the same storm and the same contractor quote. The homeowner thought they had full coverage.

What to check: look for a roof payment schedule or an ACV roof endorsement. Many policies cover the house at RC and the roof at ACV. Ask your agent in writing how roof and contents claims settle, and have them quote the RC upgrade so you can compare.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the difference between actual cash value and replacement cost in homeowners insurance?

I'm an independent insurance broker, and this is the clause I most often see hurt people after a big claim.

Actual cash value (ACV) pays what your damaged property was worth at the moment of loss. That means the replacement price minus depreciation for age and wear. Replacement cost (RC) pays to repair or replace it with new material of like kind and quality, with no depreciation taken off.

On a small claim the difference is minor. On a large one it is enormous. In 2022 I worked with a homeowner in Edmond, Oklahoma whose 17-year-old roof was destroyed by golf-ball hail. The replacement estimate was $21,400. The roof was covered at ACV, so the check was $7,240. It was the same roof and the same storm, and the gap of more than $14,000 was theirs to cover.

A few things worth knowing:

1. Your policy can cover the house at RC and the roof at ACV. Many carriers add a roof payment schedule or an ACV roof endorsement, especially in hail-prone states. Read the endorsements, not just the declarations page.

2. RC often pays in two stages. You get the ACV amount first, then the withheld depreciation once the repair is done and you submit receipts, usually within a deadline. If you miss the deadline, you can lose that money.

3. Contents are a separate question. Personal property is often covered at ACV unless you add replacement cost on contents.

4. Price both options. Ask your agent to quote ACV and RC side by side, and weigh the premium difference against what a total loss would actually pay.

In short, find the words "actual cash value" in your policy before a storm finds them for you.
